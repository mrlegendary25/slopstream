# Why Slopstream

## People have become the clipboard between their agents

You ask your agent something. You copy its answer to a teammate on Slack. They paste it into *their* agent, then copy the answer back to you.

At work it's the same with documents. Someone's agent writes a proposal and it goes into Google Drive. A colleague downloads it, gives it to their own agent to review with their own rules, and pastes the feedback back. The first person feeds it to their agent again.

The agents never meet. Every hop costs a person's time and loses context.

> *If my job is to copy-paste between my agent and yours, why am I even there?*

## An agent is a piece of its owner's intelligence

Over months, people load their agents with their **rules, methods, standards and context**: how they review code, what their API guarantees, which risks they watch for. The agent can then check things the way its owner would, quickly, without the owner doing it.

So different people's agents carry different parts of what a team knows. Today you can only reach that:

- through the person's **time**: meetings, chat threads, review requests, or
- by **copying files** into your own agent, which loses their rules and context.

## Slopstream connects that intelligence

Your agent asks your teammate's agent directly. The question arrives in their open session, their agent answers with their context, and the answer comes back into yours. **Ask anyone's agent, not anyone's time.** Borrow your team's judgment without borrowing their calendar.

## On your terms

Connecting your agent to someone else's raises a fair question: *so my agent speaks for me?* Only as far as you allow.

> **Humans approve decisions, not messages.**

- **Your agent shares what you allow.** Today you approve every message before it's sent, and see every message before your agent acts on it.
- **Decisions still need you.** Another agent can ask, but it can't agree anything for you, and it can never make your agent take an action.
- **You see everything.** Every message both ways is visible to you and logged on your machine.
- **Nobody links to you without you.** Linking needs both people at a real terminal. An agent can't do it, whatever a message says.

**Coming next:** your agent will be able to answer *factual* questions on its own (if you turn it on for a link), through a separate read-only helper that can't change anything. You'll see every automatic answer in a digest. Proposals will need both people to accept, and actions will never be triggered by another agent.

## What it isn't

Not a chatbot network, not a public inbox for your agent, and not agents running off on their own. Slopstream connects specific sessions of people who agreed to connect them, and those people stay in charge.
